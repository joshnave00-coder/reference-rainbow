How to use Reference Rainbow

Full guide

What you're looking at

Every arc is one cross-reference: a place where a study Bible says “compare this verse with that one.” There are 344,756 of them, from the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge via OpenBible.info. The grey bars along the bottom are the 1,189 chapters of the Bible from Genesis (left) to Revelation (right); each bar is as long as its chapter has verses. The taller an arc, the farther apart the two passages it joins.

Exploring

Hover a bar

Controls

Color
Distance: chapters apart (Harrison's scheme). Book: which book lists the reference. Years: how many years apart the two books were written.
Dating
Shown with Years. Traditional uses ancient Jewish and Christian attributions; Critical uses mainstream critical scholarship.
Axis
Give each chapter, or each verse, the same width.
Books
Tick testaments, categories (Law, Gospels, Paul's Letters…) or single books to show only their cross-references. Either end or Both ends decides whether one or both ends of an arc must be in them.
Relevance
Hide references with few reader votes. “Match Harrison's 63,779” sets it near the original's arc count, which is also where it starts.
Ink
How strongly overlapping arcs build up.
Translation
The English Bible used for verse text: choose it in the controls or at the top of any chapter panel. Which translations, and why not the NIV or NKJV.
Red letters
Red: words of Jesus, as marked in red-letter editions. Dark red: words of God, detected automatically from quotations — a best effort, not an editorial marking.
Theme
Light, dark, or follow your device.

Relevance votes

Every cross-reference carries a vote score from OpenBible.info: a starting score from its topical and citation data, then readers' up and down votes. The slider keeps only references at or above the score you choose. Scores show popularity with readers, not scholarly weight, and famous verses collect the most. How votes work, with the numbers.

About the dates

When and by whom each book was written is often debated. Each book lists a traditional view and a critical view (and notable alternatives), with who holds them and the ancient or modern works they come from. For coloring, a book is placed at the midpoint of its date range, so the years are approximate by design.
